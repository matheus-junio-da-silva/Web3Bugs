# Formal Verification of Celo Governance Protocol — structured report companion

**Original report:** *Formal Verification of Celo Governance Protocol*, Certora / Celo, 14 pages (May 2020 publication). Authors: Thomas Bernardi, Nurit Dor, Anastasia Fedotov, Shelly Grossman, Alexander Nutz, Lior Oppenheim, Or Pistiner, Mooly Sagiv, John Toman and James Wilcox.

**Preserved primary document:** [CeloMay2020.pdf](../resources/9/CeloMay2020.pdf) ([source in PropertyGPT](https://github.com/matheus-junio-da-silva/PropertyGPT/blob/main/certora_projects/celo_governance/CeloMay2020.pdf)). This Markdown supplies a detailed, section-by-section, **paraphrased reconstruction of the report's factual statements, tables, mathematical conditions and qualifications**. It is not the original wording and cannot replace the PDF where exact typography, quantification or notation matters. Page references use **1-based PDF page numbers**; the report's check marks denote its stated high-level properties, **not individually identified results for the 61 properties in the catalog**.

## 1. Summary (PDF pp. 1–3)

Certora and cLabs jointly constructed specifications in **Specify**, and used Certora Prover with Z3 to check the protocol implementations against those specifications. The report describes continuous integration of these checks to detect regressions.

The reported engagement had two historical stages:

| Stage | Scope as described | Dates or monitored source revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | Earlier protocol implementation; rules continued to be used after later changes | July 30–August 25, 2019; commit `9d2ec0d9399a6959565062f57efa037d03fd4fbc` |
| Second | Substantial revisions, including replacement of BondedDeposits by Accounts and LockedGold, with Election and ReleaseGold verification | Monitored commit `a46ce55ebd3867fa69290bf3be040846d433525d` |

These are commits identified by the **report**, not proof that the PropertyGPT source snapshot or all its harness variants represent those revisions. The report describes discovered defects, their subsequent fixes, and checks performed after fixing the high-severity issues. Its statements of verification apply to **the specified conditions and historical execution configuration**.

### Table 1 — main reported issues (PDF pp. 2–3)

The categories, affected components, severity and response below preserve the source's substantive information while paraphrasing its wording.

| Reported issue | Affected component | Reported mechanism | Severity | Response reported |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| List ordering can be broken | SortedLinkedList | Inconsistent neighbor identifiers supplied to insertion defeat sorted order. | High | Validate the neighbor arguments against the actual list. |
| Election front-running and asset loss | Election vote activation/revocation | Integer rounding when converting assets to voting units can redistribute value or cause losses. | High | Store and calculate voting units with greater precision. |
| Assets omitted from grant balance | ReleaseGold | Pending withdrawals are left out of the total, causing a premature termination/loss risk. | High | Include the pending withdrawal balance. |
| Invalid voting state for a group | Election reward distribution | Rewards could be attributed to a group without active votes. | Low | Add Solidity checks to protect even against future clients; the report says the then-current Go client could not trigger the condition. |
| Redundant arithmetic | Election | A subtraction uses a value that is always zero. | Low | Remove the redundant computation. |
| Unused structures | LockedGold | Nested structures are present but not used. | Low | Remove the dead code. |
| Multiplication precision | FixidityLib | Scaling fractional components down before multiplication can lose meaningful precision. | Low | Multiply fractional components without the unnecessary preliminary scaling; the report notes their product cannot overflow in that context. |

### Technology and disclaimer (PDF p. 3)

Rules invoke contract operations, define required preconditions, and express postconditions as logical verification conditions solved by Z3. Failure may be accompanied by a concrete counterexample. Formal success says only that the *given specification* is satisfied under the analyzed model: omitted properties, alternative contract versions, and assumptions outside that model are not covered. The report expressly does not constitute a complete guarantee of system security and contains a no-warranty disclaimer.

## 2. High-level specification (PDF pp. 4–11)

**Interpretation:** A check mark in the report identifies a high-level requirement described as checked. The notation below reproduces the **mathematical content**, not a source-CVL declaration or a claim about any specific extracted example. Here `Next` denotes the post-operation state, `Once`, `Until`, and `Eventually` are temporal operators, and `~` expresses observational/program equivalence from a common initial state.

### 2.1 Accounts (PDF pp. 4–5)

The model distinguishes account addresses, wallet recipients, authorized-by pointers, and three signer roles (attestation, voting, validation). The interface mentioned is:

| Operation / observation | Described meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `isAccount(x)` | Whether x is an account. |
| `walletAddress(x)` | Wallet destination for x. |
| `authorizedBy(x)` | Account that authorized address x. |
| `signerOftype(ty,x)` | Signer of role ty that x authorizes. |
| `addSigner(ty,x,d)` | Add address d as a signer of role ty for account x. |

The checked requirements are:

1. **Account existence:** A nonzero wallet or role-specific signer of x entails `isAccount(x)` under the source's nonzero/distinct-address premises.
2. **Authorization originates from an account:** If a nonzero address d has a nonzero `authorizedBy(d)`, the authorizer is an existing account. An existing account d, when already `authorizedBy(d)=x`, may only be authorized by itself (`x=d`).
3. **Signers point back to their accounts:** `signerOftype(ty,x)=d` entails `authorizedBy(d)=x` for the report's admissible addresses.
4. **Uniqueness:** If the same d is a signer of account x and account y (even for different signer roles), then `x=y`.
5. **Multiple distinct signers:** Starting from distinct, nonzero x, d and d' and different roles ty and ty', authorizing each signer should leave both with `authorizedBy(d)=authorizedBy(d')=x`.
6. **Persistence:** After an account x authorizes d, the authorization pointer cannot change under subsequent contract operations.

Representative quantified relations, with the report's restrictions on zero and identical addresses understood:

    signerOftype(ty, x) = d  =>  authorizedBy(d) = x
    signerOftype(ty, x) = d AND signerOftype(ty', y) = d  =>  x = y
    authorizedBy(d) = x  =>  Next(authorizedBy(d)) = x

The report uses Hoare triples for the multiple-signer sequencing statement. Its quantification and premises matter: none of these isolated formulas is an unrestricted proof of every access-control policy.

### 2.2 LockedGold (PDF pp. 5–7)

LockedGold holds nonspendable CELO, maintains per-account nonvoting holdings and pending withdrawals, and exposes lock, unlock, and withdraw operations. Its described observations include `totalNonvotingLockedGold()`, `nonvotingLockedGold(account)`, `pendingWithdrawals(account,i)`, `totalPendingWithdrawals(account)`, `pendingReleased(account,i)`, `totalBalance(account)` (including available funds, nonvoting locked funds and pending withdrawals), and `unlockingPeriod()`.

| Requirement | Report-level condition and scope |
| --- | --- |
| Aggregate accounting | Total nonvoting locked gold equals the sum of individual accounts' nonvoting amounts. |
| Conservation | Each account's `totalBalance` remains unchanged after LockedGold operations, **except** `slash`, `decrementNonVotingAccount`, and `incrementNonVotingAccountBalance`. |
| Isolation | An operation initiated by a different address b does not alter a's nonvoting balance under the stated `a != b` precondition. |
| Timing | After an unlock of amount x, locked plus pending gold must remain at least x **until** that withdrawal is released. This is a temporal statement, not a direct blanket assertion that every premature `withdraw` reverts. |
| Eventual availability | A recorded pending withdrawal total x is eventually withdrawable, according to the report's temporal abstraction. |
| Valid-account restriction | A positive pending withdrawal total implies `Accounts.isAccount(account)`. This explicitly crosses a contract boundary. |

Representative expressions:

    totalNonvotingLockedGold = SUM_account nonvotingLockedGold(account)
    totalBalance(a) = x  =>  Next(totalBalance(a)) = x       [except named operations]
    Once(i = unlock(account, x))
      =>  x <= nonvotingLockedGold(account) + totalPendingWithdrawals(account)
          Until pendingReleased(account, i)
    totalPendingWithdrawals(account) = x  =>  Eventually withdraw(account, x)
    totalPendingWithdrawals(account) > 0  =>  Accounts.isAccount(account)

The report distinguishes state preservation from eventual availability and uses temporal operators explicitly; they should not be collapsed into a one-transaction assertion.

### 2.3 Elections (PDF pp. 7–8)

Election maintains group voting units, group assets including rewards, voting-unit ownership, pending votes, and account-level values. The report defines `unitsForGroup(group)`, `votesForGroup(group)`, `unitsForGroupByAccount(group,account)`, `votesForGroupByAccount(group,account)`, `pendingVotesForGroup(group)`, `pendingVotesForGroupByAccount(group,account)` and `totalBalance(account)`. An operation `revokeActive(group,account,value)` removes an asset value of active votes.

The checked conditions include group totals matching per-account sums (units, assets and pending votes), `unitsForGroup(g) <= votesForGroup(g)`, a zero-unit group having zero assets, and equivalence between holding all of a group's units and holding all of its assets. The report specifically links the zero-units/zero-assets rule to the **low-severity election reward-state defect**. Account `totalBalance` should not decrease across operations except for `forceDecrementVotes`.

    unitsForGroup(g) = SUM_a unitsForGroupByAccount(g, a)
    votesForGroup(g) = SUM_a votesForGroupByAccount(g, a)
    pendingVotesForGroup(g) = SUM_a pendingVotesForGroupByAccount(g, a)
    unitsForGroup(g) <= votesForGroup(g)
    unitsForGroup(g) = 0  =>  votesForGroup(g) = 0
    (unitsForGroupByAccount(g,a) = unitsForGroup(g))
       <=> (votesForGroupByAccount(g,a) = votesForGroup(g))
    totalBalance(a) <= Next(totalBalance(a))                [except forceDecrementVotes]
    revokeActive(g,a,x); revokeActive(g,a,y) ~ revokeActive(g,a,x+y)

The final relation compares two *program executions from the same initial state*; it does not mean the two invocations are interchangeable under arbitrary differing initial states.

### 2.4 Release Gold (PDF pp. 8–10)

This contract manages a grant whose assets become releasable over time. The report names `withdraw(x)`, `totalWithdrawn()`, `totalBalance()`, `totalReward()`, `releasedTotalAmount()`, and `MAX_WITHDRAWL` (**source spelling retained**).

| Requirement | Content and explicit qualification |
| --- | --- |
| Withdrawal additivity | Two withdrawals x then y should be equivalent to one withdrawal x+y from the same initial state. |
| Maximum withdrawal | Successful withdrawal x is bounded by the grant maximum plus rewards; **slashing is an exception**. |
| Eventual access | Positive grant balance not already withdrawn should eventually be withdrawable; **revocation is an exception**. |
| No premature withdrawal | A successful withdrawal may not exceed released but not-yet-withdrawn funds. |
| Monotone total balance | The total cannot decrease across operations, **except slashing**. The report connects this property directly to its ReleaseGold asset-loss finding. |
| Monotone release amount | `releasedTotalAmount()` must not decrease over time. |

    withdraw(x); withdraw(y) ~ withdraw(x+y)
    withdraw(x) => totalWithdrawn() + x <= MAX_WITHDRAWL + totalReward()
    0 < x <= totalBalance() - totalWithdrawn() => Eventually withdraw(x)
    withdraw(x) => x <= releasedTotalAmount() - totalWithdrawn()
    totalBalance() <= Next(totalBalance())                       [except slashing]
    releasedTotalAmount() <= Next(releasedTotalAmount())

**Important:** the report uses temporal liveness and exceptions. The present PropertyGPT directory does not contain a matching set of ReleaseGold source rules under the Celo Governance specs used to create Project 9's 61 examples.

### 2.5 LinkedList and SortedLinkedList (PDF pp. 10–11)

The reported checked targets were sorted list implementations keyed by bytes32, uint256 and address, with the discussion noting possible adaptation to unsorted variants. Observations include `contains(key)`, `greater(key)`, `lesser(key)`, `head`, `tail` and `numElements`. The source displays the conditions using its chosen conventions for the head and tail of the list.

| Category | Conditions described |
| --- | --- |
| Boundary consistency | Membership and zero-valued predecessor/successor pointers must agree with head/tail; an empty list has zero head/tail; a nonempty list has present nonzero boundaries. |
| Reserved key | Key zero is never a stored element. |
| Sorted order | A member's value lies between the values at tail and head (using the report's ordering convention). |
| Insert/update observations | Written value matches the requested value; next/previous pointers target zero or existing elements and maintain mutual adjacency. |
| Insertion prerequisites | Nonzero new key; not already present; distinct from the supplied neighbors; capacity remaining; neighbor-existence conditions; nonzero `msg.value`; and at least one *correct* neighbor relative to the actual ordering. |
| Removal | Only existing keys are removable; removal clears the key/value, reconnects its neighbors and leaves unrelated nested neighbors and other elements unchanged. |
| Noninterference | Updates to one element do not alter unrelated elements' values or break sortedness. |

The report's concrete schematic relations include:

    head = 0 <=> numElements = 0
    head = 0 <=> tail = 0
    NOT contains(0)
    contains(k) => value(tail) <= value(k) <= value(head)
    key.next.prev = key ; key.prev.next = key                [for nonzero real keys]
    after remove(k): NOT contains(k) AND value(k) = 0

The insertion prerequisite list in the original uses the predicate spelling `contained(lesser)`/`contained(greater)` as printed there, whereas the attribute description uses `contains`; this discrepancy is **not silently normalized**. The original contains a `msg.value != 0` prerequisite that is preserved here as a report fact rather than inferred to be correct for every implementation.

### 2.6 StableToken (PDF p. 11)

For the ERC20-based cUSD token, the report states conformance to Certora's standard ERC20 specification only under an explicit simplification: **both the inflation factor and the inflation rate are kept fixed at 1**. This qualification is essential; unconditional ERC20 equivalence is not claimed.

## 3. High-severity finding explanations (PDF pp. 12–13)

### 3.1 SortedLinkedList

The insert operation previously accepted bad `lesserKey`/`greaterKey` hints, permitting a list position inconsistent with the actual sorted order; a fix was checked afterward. The report also warns that clients of `AddressLinkedList` or `AddressSortedLinkedList` must use the address-aware wrapper rather than an internal key-oriented insertion that skips conversion to bytes32.

### 3.2 Election — precision, rounding and asset redistribution

The described conversion model holds `groupAssets`, `totalVotingUnits` and individual voter units:

    unitsToVotes(u) = groupAssets * u / totalVotingUnits
    votesToUnits(x) = totalVotingUnits * x / groupAssets

Solidity integer division rounds down. The report illustrates the resulting transfer of value with a 50-unit, 200-asset starting group (including 150 rewards):

| Event | A's units / asset value | B's units / asset value | Group units | Group assets |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Initial state | 10 / 40 | 40 / 160 | 50 | 200 |
| A withdraws 19 assets | The conversion rounds 4.75 units down to 4 | Unchanged unit count | — | — |
| After withdrawal | 6 / 23 (rather than 21) | 40 / 157 (rather than 160) | 46 | 181 |

The source interprets the first path as A receiving an extra 2 assets at the expense of other group members. An independent deposit path starts with C contributing 19 to a group of 200 assets/50 units. Rounding assigns **4 units rather than approximately 4.68**; afterward the group holds 219 assets and 54 units. A conversion of C's 4 units back to assets returns **floor(4 × 219 / 54) = 16**, i.e. a 3-asset loss relative to the deposit. The reported remediation is higher-precision voting units. These are report examples, **not Prover counterexamples tied to the catalog's Governance rule names**.

### 3.3 ReleaseGold — pending withdrawals omitted

`getTotalBalance()` is intended to include property controlled through LockedGold and Election. When pending LockedGold withdrawals were ignored in that calculation, the grant contract could believe its assets were exhausted and clear the refund address. A revoked beneficiary's payout could therefore leave pending assets without a refund recipient. The source says the correction counted the entire pending-withdrawal queue in ReleaseGold's total.

## 4. Conclusion (PDF p. 14)

The report concludes that the verification effort exposed and helped fix defects in earlier implementations, strengthened confidence **within the modeled rules**, and placed checks into Celo's CI process to reduce future regressions. It does not offer an unrestricted security guarantee.

## Catalog-specific attribution and fidelity notes

- **Different property populations:** The PDF presents Accounts, LockedGold, Election, ReleaseGold, linked-list and StableToken *high-level specifications*, whereas Project 9 catalogs **61 active CVL rules/invariants** from ten PropertyGPT files, including old Governance variants, harness-specific probes and Reserve. Neither the PDF's check marks nor issue table provides per-source-rule/per-harness/per-commit results for all 61.
- **Results:** Accordingly the 61 catalog entries retain `Result=NOT_STATED` and `Detection Outcome=NOT_STATED` without fabricating `VERIFIED`, `VIOLATED`, or `FINDING`. The seven historical issues remain separately documented in `results/bugs.csv`.
- **Implementation differences:** Historical scripts use `--optimistic_loop` (and Accounts `--loop_iter 2`), special Solidity harnesses and source transforms. A Governance harness replaces dequeuing with a no-op. Source transformations affect arithmetic, proposal approval and external calls. These facts restrict any inference from the report's aggregate prose to the source snapshot here.
- **Source reproduction:** This Markdown is a detailed, data-faithful companion, **not verbatim report text**. The preserved original PDF is decisive for exact status symbols, graphics, pages, mathematical typography and historical wording. No historical prover run was executed to construct this companion.
