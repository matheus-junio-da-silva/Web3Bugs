## Motivation

Test withdrawal independence from prior operations involving a different operator.

## Reconstructed methodology

The following steps are reconstructed from the literal CVL assertion, local Solidity snapshot and public report; they do not claim knowledge of private auditor reasoning.

1. The property sets up the specified environments, guards and/or initial state.
2. Execute a baseline withdraw and store `succSceanrio1`, but never use this success flag in the assertion; execute an arbitrary `f` that changes at least one state measure of `otherOperator`, then retry withdraw. The rule does **not** restrict `eWithdraw.msg.sender` to operator/owner or require the baseline withdrawal to succeed. Its selector-free `f` is only constrained to change some state of `otherOperator`, **not** constrained to avoid changing the originally tested operator as well.
3. A violation occurs on *any* second-scenario withdrawal revert, including a normal access-control, amount, or delegation-lock rejection unrelated to the first scenario.
4. This tests only the encoded conditions; any rule branch/filter and assumptions must be retained and not overgeneralized to unrestricted real calls.

## Why the Solidity excerpt is relevant

The target withdraw first requires `msg.sender == operator || msg.sender == ownerOf(operator)` and then a passed delegation lock. Those checks in `FullyBackedBonding.sol:156-173`, plus the unbonded amount check in `AbstractBonding.sol:329-342`, explain why the unrestricted second scenario can revert without another operator causing interference. Other-operator bond/deposit methods can mutate ledgers, but the assertion does not isolate their causal effect. The cited source ranges are literal slices of the archived snapshot; the imports are not presumed to be present merely because their Solidity names appear. The CVL Sload hook monitors `operatorHolderRefToBondID` and Sstore hooks synthesize aggregate ghost values where needed; the harness `registerBondID` has its mapping assignment commented out, despite the production `getBondID` using keccak256.
