## Motivation

Test a conditional revert implication: if the **post-call** `inPostSlashingPeriod()` getter is true, the tracked stake must have reverted. This is weaker than directly requiring a pre-call post-slashing state.

## Reconstructed methodology

The following is reconstructed from the observable CVL property, available Solidity/harness code, and report. It is not a claim about private auditor reasoning.

1. Invoke `stake@withrevert` with no required pre-call value of `inPostSlashingPeriod`.
2. Store `lastReverted`.
3. Evaluate `inPostSlashingPeriod()` **after** the call and assert that it implies `lastReverted`.

A counterexample needs a **successful** stake with the flag still true at the post-call observation. If a hypothetical successful stake started in a post-slashing state but **cleared** the flag, the implication would not fail. Furthermore, any revert reason satisfies the consequent. In the supplied Solidity `_stake` itself guards the flag and does not clear it, but that concrete behavior is not encoded in the CVL antecedent; do not generalize the rule to every possible implementation.

## Why the Solidity excerpt is relevant

The `_stake` implementation contains the `SLASHING_ONGOING` guard and does not clear `inPostSlashingPeriod` on a successful stake path. This explains why a post-state predicate is meaningful for the supplied implementation; it does **not** transform the historical post-call assertion into an explicit pre-state condition.
